# CQ Diggings Queensland High-Grade Gold Investigation

## Purpose

Build a Queensland-wide, source-grounded register of documented high-grade gold reefs, rich shoots, exceptional crushings, abandoned workings and incompletely tested extensions for CQ Diggings.

This is not a list of places where the public should dig. It is a historical and geological evidence register. Every location must retain its source, date, original wording, aliases, spatial precision and confidence level. Coordinates must not be invented from vague place descriptions.

## Investigation standard

Each candidate will be checked against as many of the following as are available:

1. Geological Survey of Queensland publications and maps.
2. Mining warden and mines department reports.
3. Queensland parliamentary papers and government gazettes.
4. Historical newspapers through Trove.
5. Mine plans, lease plans and historical cadastral maps.
6. Geoscience Australia and former BMR records.
7. University theses and peer-reviewed geological work.
8. Modern exploration reports lodged with Queensland.
9. Current mineral occurrence, tenure and disturbance mapping.

## Evidence classes

- **A - Confirmed:** official production or treatment record plus mapped mine or reef.
- **B - Strong:** named mine or reef with a credible contemporary crushing record, supported by an independent source.
- **C - Lead:** contemporary report with identifiable locality but not yet independently confirmed.
- **D - Folklore:** unsupported lost-reef, prospector or newspaper story.
- **E - Rejected:** wrong state, duplicated identity, bad OCR, demonstrably false or materially exaggerated.

## Fields required for the map and register

- record ID
- reef, mine or occurrence name
- aliases and spelling variants
- goldfield and district
- locality description in original wording
- latitude and longitude where supported
- spatial precision and coordinate source
- reef, lode, leader, shoot, cap, alluvial or other occurrence type
- strike, dip, width and reported length
- crushing tonnage
- recovered gold
- calculated grade in oz/t and g/t
- assay versus bulk crushing distinction
- visible or specimen gold description
- workings and deepest recorded level
- reason operations stopped
- extension or unresolved-target statement
- historical source and page
- geological or modern corroborating source
- evidence class
- public-access and tenure warning

## Central Queensland verification pass 2

### Critical locality correction: Kirk Diggings

The first pass incorrectly grouped Kirk Diggings with Calliope because the 1873 newspaper item appeared among Calliope search results. Queensland postal directories from 1892 to 1896 consistently list **Kirk Diggings under Ravenswood**. It is therefore not a Central Queensland Calliope record. The reported 11.5 oz/t result remains a statewide lead but must not be mapped near Calliope.

### Morinish and Blackfellow's Gully

The second pass substantially strengthens the Morinish group:

- An August 1867 field description places Kennedy's Reef about four miles from Morinish and roughly 300 or 400 yards from the Rosewood road. This is useful spatial evidence but is not precise enough for coordinates.
- A March 1868 report records 90 tons from Kennedy's Reef returning 270 oz, exactly 3 oz/t or approximately 93.3 g/t.
- Another March 1868 report gives a cumulative Kennedy's Reef recovery of 286 oz after gold previously extracted by dollying was included.
- The 1867 field account records established Alliance and Welcome reefs. The Alliance prospecting shaft was about 120 ft deep, while later geological descriptions state that Welcome was worked beyond 350 ft.
- The authoritative 1897 Queensland geological summary names Homeward Bound, Carnarvon Castle and Mary Florence and describes the Welcome Reef as quartz containing iron pyrite, arsenical pyrite, minor galena and very minor zinc blende in fine-grained serpentinous greywacke.
- An 1898 field and quarterly-return article records 634 tons treated at Morinish for 438 oz during 1890, an average of about 0.691 oz/t or 21.5 g/t across the recorded ore.

The 1898 quarterly table provides named parcel results that require image-level checking because of OCR ambiguity:

| Mine or claim | Stone treated | Gold shown in OCR | Calculated grade | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Alliance Extended United | 202 t | OCR appears to show 96 oz | about 0.475 oz/t or 14.8 g/t if 96 oz is correct | provisional pending page-image check |
| Connors Relief No. 1 South-East | 17 t | 11 oz 19 dwt | about 0.703 oz/t or 21.9 g/t | strong contemporary bulk return |
| Rainbow prospecting claim | 1 t 6 cwt | 4 oz 18 dwt | about 3.77 oz/t or 117 g/t | high-grade small parcel |
| Rosewood Champion | 4 t | 68 oz 16 dwt | about 17.2 oz/t or 535 g/t | exceptional result; must be verified against page image |

The same report states:

- Connors Relief had an approximately 10-inch reef at the 90 ft shaft level, with selected sections prospecting at 2 oz 10 dwt to 3 to 5 oz/t.
- Carnarvon Castle's previous crushing returned 6 oz/t, although the tonnage was not given in the retrieved text.
- Jubilee Extended reportedly exposed a rich shoot at about 100 ft depth.

This confirms that Blackfellow's Gully contained genuinely rich, narrow shoots. It does not establish that the entire reefs carried those grades.

### Calliope

The Calliope evidence is now better separated into named reefs:

- John Bull Reef: 32 tons reportedly yielded 47 oz in July 1872, about 1.47 oz/t or 45.7 g/t.
- Catherine Reef: the same report gives approximately 1 oz 10 dwt per ton, about 46.7 g/t.
- John Bull later produced a much lower but still payable parcel of 13 tons for 6 oz 8 dwt in 1889, about 0.49 oz/t or 15.3 g/t.
- H. Kirkham's Reef produced 11 oz from 12 tons in 1889, about 0.92 oz/t or 28.5 g/t.
- The Calliope Gold Mining Company was sinking to intersect Baker's vein in March 1873. An August report says it was following a run from Baker's old shaft with splendid results, but the retrieved text does not provide a defensible bulk grade.

The official 1885 Calliope Goldfield geology map is confirmed as GSQ map **mr008919**, at one mile to one inch. The Gladstone Mining District mine-map dataset is **mr001908**. These are the preferred spatial sources for resolving John Bull, Baker's vein, Catherine Reef and H. Kirkham's Reef rather than geocoding newspaper descriptions.

## Central Queensland verification pass 3

### Mount Cassidy and Jerry's Gully

Mount Cassidy is now a priority CQ Diggings research record because it can be reconstructed from contemporary technical and cadastral evidence.

The discovery was made by **A. J. Clunes, J. Noonan and M. J. Noonan** at Jerry's Gully. The contemporary locality description is:

- northern half of portion 20v
- parish of Aricia
- county of Pakington
- about three miles south-west of Bannockburn homestead
- about eight miles west of the western boundary of the old Morinish Goldfield
- about 25 road miles from Ridgelands and 45 road miles from Rockhampton

This is suitable for cadastral reconstruction. It is not converted to a modern coordinate until the historical portion plan and current parcel framework are reconciled.

The discovery article described:

- a north-south mineralised lode opened intermittently for about 90 yards
- a reported width of 8 to 9 ft
- many shallow trial holes and shafts
- visible fine gold in quartz and iron-rich material
- a claimed connection toward the lost Caroline Reef near Craigilee homestead
- selected assays of 4 oz 6 dwt/t and 14 oz/t

Those selected assays were not representative bulk grades. A preliminary crushing at Gracemere subsequently returned **0.5 oz/t of free gold**, approximately **15.6 g/t**. This is still high-grade by modern bulk-mining standards but far below the promotional selected assays.

Government Geologist **J. H. Reid** inspected the occurrence and described it as an unusual lode rather than an ordinary reef. He considered systematic trenching, shaft sinking into the sulphide zone and crosscutting warranted. He specifically warned against installing a small battery merely to pick out the rich surface ore, because that could prejudice proper evaluation of a larger lower-grade deposit.

The published technical source is J. H. Reid, **Gold discovery at Mount Cassidy, near Morinish, Rockhampton district**, *Queensland Government Mining Journal*, volume 32, number 368, January 1931, with map. This publication and its map are now a high-priority acquisition.

For a Manticore-focused historical layer, Mount Cassidy is relevant because it had shallow workings, visible surface gold, specimen material and a defined cadastral location. It must nevertheless be shown with current-tenure, land-access and disturbance warnings. The evidence does not establish public detecting access.

### New Zealand Gully, Cawarral and Mount Archer

Additional small hard-rock leads were identified:

- Harris and party's reef at New Zealand Gully reportedly treated 54 tons for 51 oz in 1872. The calculated grade is about 0.94 oz/t or 29.4 g/t.
- An 1868 New Zealand Gully report records one reef parcel returning 3 oz/t, while a separate 10-ton Canadian parcel returned 0.5 oz/t. The reef name attached to the 3 oz/t result needs recovery from the complete article.
- The North Star on the summit of Mount Archer was reported in an 1876 mineral-resources account as having previously returned 4.5 oz/t. Tonnage was not supplied in the retrieved text.
- At the Cawarral machine in 1884, OCR appears to record 80 tons from New Greeks Reef for 50 oz 3 dwt and 20 tons from Elena Reef for 8 oz 2 dwt. These calculate to approximately 19.5 g/t and 12.6 g/t respectively, but the first tonnage must be checked against the page image.

Harris and party's reef is the strongest of this group because it has a substantial bulk parcel rather than a specimen assay.

### Crocodile goldfield

Two high-grade Crocodile records now require name resolution:

- A March 1897 report states that 40 tons from a newly discovered reef yielded 120 oz, exactly 3 oz/t or approximately 93.3 g/t.
- An 1886 field report records a mine returning 4 oz/t and discusses the Union, Hibernian and Last Chance operations, but the search extract does not securely attach the 4 oz/t return to a mine name.

These cannot yet be mapped as separate reefs. The next check is the complete warden table or original newspaper page, followed by the Crocodile mine map.

### Clermont status

The Clermont sweep confirmed multiple named leads but did not yet produce enough clean bulk-return data for promotion:

- Brothers Reef: an early 1905 report mentions a return of 61 oz 12 dwt, but the treated tonnage is unreadable in the search extract.
- Fiddler's or Fidder's Creek: a November 1900 report describes a 120 ft shaft and a 3 ft payable reef.
- Gibraltar Consolidated: an April 1900 prospectus describes several quartz reefs assaying 13 to 15 dwt/t, about 20.2 to 23.3 g/t. These are assays, not bulk crushings.
- The 1897 Clermont field account reports local workings returning about 25 dwt/t, but the mine names and tonnages need extraction from the complete article.

The official **Clermont Goldfield Contour Map, 1902, mr008980** remains the preferred map framework. Clermont stays in the active queue rather than being represented by unsupported grades.

## Central Queensland verification pass 4

### Raglan: Port Alma, Duke of Brittany and Mount Holly

Raglan has produced the most visually spectacular lead in this pass, but the evidence is uneven.

- A 1924 district reminiscence places the **Duke of Brittany** reefing claim on the hillside opposite Raglan railway station, about a quarter of a mile from the hotel and station. It says the mine had previously been worked profitably and was expected to reopen.
- The same account places the **Port Alma reef** over the range from the Duke of Brittany. It attributes the discovery to two men employed by Mr Sinclair, known as Barefooted Bob and Charlie King, and describes a very small, extraordinarily rich reef in which strings of gold held the quartz together.
- This is compelling specimen-gold language, not a measured grade. The account was written decades after the discovery, so Port Alma remains class C until a contemporary crushing, lease or warden record is recovered.
- A contemporary 1880 statement concerning the **Duke of Brittany**, within half a mile of Fire Creek, claimed that more than £3,000 worth of gold had been taken during the preceding two years. The retrieved text does not give tonnage, so no grade can be calculated.
- A 1924 locality account places shallow surface gold about 2.5 miles up Fire Creek and records a reported 56 oz nugget from the slope above the principal gully. This is alluvial evidence and must not be represented as a reef grade.
- At **Mount Holly**, an 1887 company report says 100 tons were contract-crushed at Bennett's Raglan mill but the result was unsatisfactory. The company later re-found the old reef beyond a sandstone bar: it was reported as a four-foot lode showing gold through the stone and enlarging northward. The actual gold return from the 100-ton parcel was not stated in the retrieved text.

For a Manticore historical layer, Port Alma is a high-interest specimen-gold lead and Duke of Brittany is the best spatial anchor. Neither should receive an invented coordinate. The preferred reconstruction is the old Raglan township and railway alignment, Fire Creek, the Duke of Brittany claim and the intervening range, checked against a mine or lease plan.

### Mount Larcom and Targinnie

The Mount Larcom search repeatedly resolves into the nearby **Targinnie** reefing district rather than a single Mount Larcom bonanza.

- A 1924 retrospective history identifies the **Archer reef** and the Targinnie Gold-mining Company and reports an average of about 1 oz/t, approximately 31.1 g/t.
- A 1904 field summary says production from the Targinnie mines was approaching 3,000 tons at about 1 oz/t. The wording appears to describe a district or principal-mine aggregate and needs checking against the complete warden return before being attached solely to Archer.
- A December 1903 mining report records a 20-ton parcel yielding 25 oz 18 dwt of retorted gold, about 1.295 oz/t or 40.3 g/t. The search extract does not securely preserve the mine name, so this is retained as an unnamed Targinnie parcel.
- The broader field evidence also includes a 225-ton parcel at about 10 dwt/t and references to stone yielding 2 to 3 oz/t, but the latter wording is not sufficiently connected to tonnage or mine identity in the retrieved text.

Targinnie therefore qualifies as a strong high-grade district, with at least one substantial named reef system and one clean 20-ton high-grade parcel. The next requirement is the complete 1902 to 1904 warden table and a Targinnie mine plan. Mount Larcom itself should not be given the Targinnie grades merely because it was the nearest town.

### Glassford Creek and Many Peaks

Glassford Creek is historically important but its bulk gold grades are moderate rather than fabulous.

- The Queensland Heritage Register history identifies the principal lodes as **Blue Bag** in the north and **Lady Inez** in the south.
- After a ten-head battery was erected in 1897, 560 long tons reportedly returned 166 oz gold. This is about 0.296 oz/t or 9.22 g/t.
- In 1916, Hunting and Clark sank a new shaft at Blue Bag. Treatment of 227 long tons returned 22 long tons of copper, 119 oz gold and 155 oz silver. The contained gold equates to about 0.524 oz/t or 16.3 g/t, although it was a copper-gold ore rather than a free-gold quartz parcel.
- The heritage record reports about 25,000 long tons of ore in dumps near the smelters in 1916. That historical quantity does not imply present economic value, ownership or permission to sample.
- Surviving mullock, ore dumps, possible filled shafts, tramway formation and smelter remains are heritage fabric. Queensland Parks warns that the structures and old mine workings are unstable and that visitors may view but must not enter the smelter site.

Many Peaks itself was a large auriferous copper operation developed as a flux source for Mount Morgan. The current evidence supports scale and underground continuity, including old workings to about the 470 ft level, but does not reveal a small, fabulously rich free-gold reef. It belongs in a copper-gold systems layer, not at the top of the narrow high-grade reef list.

### Diglum and Dan Dan Creek

Diglum now has a useful link between historical prospecting and modern exploration, but it is not yet a verified rich reef.

- An 1884 field report says a good deal of gold had been found on leaders at Diglum Creek. The leaders were described as heading north-west across the range toward Dan Dan Creek. No tonnage or recovered-gold figure was supplied.
- Modern Queensland exploration report **CR033781**, for EPM 11367, states that magnetite-rich rock-chip samples assaying above 20 ppm gold, equivalent to more than 20 g/t Au, were collected from the **Eldorado pit**. The same report notes albite alteration nearby.
- This is a selected rock-chip result and must not be treated as an orebody grade. It nevertheless provides modern corroboration that locally high gold values occur in the Diglum prospect area.
- Queensland report **CR033743** includes gold assay results for drill hole PDH7 and a mineralogical study of outcrop samples. These appendices are the next priority because they may show whether the high surface values persist below the pit.

Diglum is promoted from a vague historical lead to class C with modern corroboration. The north-west leader trend toward Dan Dan Creek is a testable historical orientation, but exact workings and tenure must be mapped from the report geometry and mine records before any public-facing location is published.

## Central Queensland verification pass 5

### Rosewood Champion: exceptional return confirmed

The full June-quarter 1898 gold warden report confirms that **Rosewood Champion** was not an OCR artefact. It is one of the strongest documented small hard-rock gold occurrences found in Central Queensland during this investigation.

- Owners and prospectors: **Messrs Kele and Read**.
- Historical title: prospecting protection area No. 72, comprising a 300 ft by 400 ft reward claim plus two ordinary claims, giving total ground described as 400 ft by 400 ft.
- Locality: at the head of **Five-mile Creek**, about five miles south of Rosewood township, in dense scrub and rugged country.
- Transport: the ore had to be packed to Rosewood and then carted to the Blackfellow's Gully battery, a stated journey of about 20 miles.
- Bulk crushing: **4 tons returned 68 oz 16 dwt**, equal to **17.2 oz/t or approximately 535 g/t Au**.
- Strike and form: the lode was reported to trend east-west, with little apparent dip and an almost vertical attitude. It consisted of a soft ironstone formation containing exceedingly rich veins.
- Workings: a diagonal sink had reached about **53 ft**. At approximately 45 ft, defined walls were forming and the mineralised formation was reported as roughly 2 ft 8 in to 3 ft thick.
- Internal grade variation: the wider broken formation near surface was said to prospect at 3 to 4 oz/t. Rich leaders at depth reportedly prospected at 15 to 20 oz/t. A narrower 10 in to 1 ft 3 in formation in the bottom was said to prospect at about 30 oz/t by washing. These are prospect estimates, not separate bulk crushings.
- Sulphide question: a mineral lode at least 3 ft wide was encountered on the north-west side of the bottom and described as decomposing copper and iron sulphides. An assay had been submitted but its result was not available in the report.

The combination of an official warden report, a named claim, a reproducible locality, a substantial four-ton parcel and described underground geometry promotes Rosewood Champion to **class B, bordering on A once its mine or lease plan is matched**. It is presently the clearest CQ example of an extremely rich, narrow ironstone-hosted shoot rather than a merely promotional assay.

The immediate research targets are the continuation of the east-west formation beyond the 400 ft by 400 ft historical ground, the bottom of the 53 ft sink, and the unresolved sulphide lode on its north-west side. These are historical geological questions only. The report does not establish present access or prospecting rights.

### Other Rosewood workings

The same warden report adds several nearby leads:

- **Lucky Hit**: two men were attempting to retrace a shoot of gold that had cut out during earlier work by Edwards and party. This is a direct historical unresolved-shoot statement.
- **Golden Bar**: described as having produced one or two very rich returns in Rosewood's early period, but the 1898 report gives no tonnage or gold quantity.
- **Young Caledonia** and **Golden Spec**: both had been reapplied for, indicating recognisable abandoned or dormant historical workings, but without grades in this source.

Lucky Hit is particularly relevant to the missed-shoot register because the contemporary work was explicitly aimed at relocating a lost continuation.

### Ulam

The June-quarter 1898 warden report provides several clean Ulam records:

- **Golden Spur**: 22 tons 10 cwt returned 28 oz 15 dwt, approximately **1.278 oz/t or 39.8 g/t**. The mine was being worked on tribute and was said to be languishing for want of development capital.
- **McKay and Turner's prospecting area**, in the Bajool scrub: 11 tons returned 12 oz, approximately **1.09 oz/t or 33.9 g/t**. The trial was nevertheless described as below expectations.
- **Iron Duke P.C. Extended**: 7 tons 11 cwt returned 3 oz 16 dwt, about **0.50 oz/t or 15.6 g/t**.
- **Raglan Lass**: 11 tons 10 cwt returned 4 oz 13 dwt, about **0.40 oz/t or 12.5 g/t**.
- In an abandoned shaft adjoining McPrang and Petersen's alluvial claim, Elliot and party reportedly recovered a **17 oz 16 dwt nugget** on their second day. McPrang and Petersen brought in another 16 oz 5 dwt 12 gr parcel containing a nugget of nearly 7 oz.

Golden Spur is the most defensible hard-rock Ulam candidate. The statement that it lacked capital is important because cessation was not attributed to exhaustion of the reef.

### Mount Hector

Mount Hector emerges as another strong Gladstone-district hard-rock record:

- A 1924 historical account reports that its first crushing of **100 tons averaged 1 oz 10 dwt/t**, equal to **1.5 oz/t or approximately 46.7 g/t**.
- The same account says Mount Hector was worked for about 18 months. The retrieved extract truncates before the reason for closure and therefore that reason remains unresolved.
- A 1903 report states that a tunnel in the Prospector's claim intersected the lode at about 90 ft.
- By 1906 the operation was described as a copper-gold mine. A furnace designed for 25 tons per day was being erected and selected ore was expected to carry about 3 per cent copper and 15 dwt gold per ton, approximately 23.3 g/t Au. That figure is an operating selection target, not a treatment return.

Mount Hector is class B pending recovery of the contemporary first-crushing return and mine plan. The 90 ft tunnel intersection provides a concrete working to map, while the later copper-gold phase warns against treating all material as free-milling reef gold.

### New Zealand Gully and Cawarral additions

The same 1898 warden report strengthens two previously identified records:

- **Victoria Extended**, New Zealand Gully: 4 tons returned 4 oz 5 dwt, or about **1.06 oz/t and 33.0 g/t**. The battery owner said the stone was heavily mineralised and inadequately treated by his plant, implying possible unrecovered value.
- **Wonga Extended**, New Zealand Gully: 132 tons returned 187 oz 11 dwt 5 gr, approximately **1.421 oz/t or 44.2 g/t**. The mine was working a reported three-foot reef estimated at 1.5 to 2 oz/t.
- **Long Tunnel**, Cawarral: 40 tons returned 21 oz 3 dwt, approximately **0.529 oz/t or 16.4 g/t**.
- **Helena Extended United**: 360 tons returned 169 oz 2 dwt, approximately **0.470 oz/t or 14.6 g/t**. Separately, 70 tons of Helena tailings yielded 41 oz 16 dwt by cyanide, showing substantial gold had remained after the original battery treatment.

Wonga Extended is now the strongest New Zealand Gully bulk record because its high grade was sustained across 132 tons. Victoria Extended is an important metallurgical lead because the contemporary mill owner explicitly considered the treatment inadequate.

### Statewide benchmark: Mount Britten

Although outside the CQ core, Mount Britten provides an important Queensland comparison and contains remarkably rich documented reef and alluvial occurrences.

- **Little Wanderer** recorded successive parcels of 185 tons for 866 oz, 100 tons for 590 oz and 136 tons for 960 oz. These calculate to approximately **4.68 oz/t, 5.90 oz/t and 7.06 oz/t** respectively.
- **Wanderer Union**, also described as East and West, returned 42 oz from 12 tons, or **3.5 oz/t**.
- **Evelyn Reef** was reported at 3 oz/t from 25 tons.
- The district account records nuggets of 40, 45, 50 and 69 oz in Nuggety Gully, a 70 oz nugget from Webley's claim, and rich quartz specimens including one containing about 100 oz gold.
- Near Mount Clarke, a group of little-worked reefs was specifically identified as deserving extended trial. Government Geologist R. L. Jack was reported as considering that the best part of the Mount Britten field might yet be found there.

Mount Britten is compelling evidence that Queensland did contain genuine multi-tonne reef parcels running several ounces per ton. It also demonstrates why exceptional small-field records require mine plans and geological reconstruction rather than relying only on modern mine databases.

## Central Queensland verification pass 6

### Mount Usher: historical bonanza mine with modern corroboration

Mount Usher is promoted to the top tier of the CQ investigation because its rich historical production is now supported by modern geochemical work and a coherent structural target.

- An 1897 company report states that the last 80 tons raised had yielded **more than 3 oz/t**, or more than **93.3 g/t Au**.
- The same report described the reef as expected to traverse the full length of the property. This was a forward-looking company statement rather than proof of continuity, but it records the historical geological interpretation.
- A February 1899 cable reported 11 tons 10 cwt from the No. 5 level. The OCR of the recovered-gold figure is ambiguous and must be checked against the page image before that parcel is entered as a grade.
- A 1903 alluvial discovery on the Dee River near Mount Usher, worked by Greene, Roy and Young, reportedly produced a **62 oz 10 dwt slug** and two additional pieces of about 9 oz. This is alluvial or eluvial evidence and must remain separate from the reef-production record.
- A 2020 ASX technical summary reported approximately **100,000 oz of historical alluvial and hard-rock production** at Mount Usher and described a concealed magnetic high rimmed by historical workings at the junction of two major structural lineaments. This is a company exploration interpretation, not a declared mineral resource.
- In December 2025, re-assaying of **125 historical rock-chip pulps** collected in 2017 returned 19 results above 1 g/t Au. Reported standout values included **24.1, 16.7, 15.2 and 14.5 g/t Au**. These remain selected surface samples rather than drill intersections.
- The samples cover approximately **3.8 km of strike** along historically mined shear-hosted quartz-vein lodes, including the Mount Usher, Caledonia-Anglo Saxon and Victor mine areas.
- The modern interpretation is a base-metal-gold-telluride system formed at sub-epithermal depths and related to a deeper quartz-feldspar porphyry system. Elevated silver, base metals, molybdenum, gallium and tellurium were reported with the gold.

This is unusually strong continuity between historical production and modern exploration. It also means Mount Usher is not an abandoned public prospecting area: the company reported that **MDL 2020** had been granted and that magnetic, mapping, electromagnetic and possible drilling work was planned. Any CQ Diggings presentation must show the modern tenure and avoid implying detecting or sampling access.

### Barmundoo and Mount Rainbow: two exceptional refractory-ore records

The 1895 Gladstone gold warden report identifies Barmundoo as one of the richest overlooked groups in this investigation.

- **Mabel prospecting claim**: 20 tons yielded **181 oz**, equal to **9.05 oz/t or approximately 281.5 g/t Au**.
- **Sea View P.C. Extended**: 13 tons yielded approximately **57.5 oz**, equal to **4.42 oz/t or about 137.6 g/t Au**.
- Approximately 5.5 tons of tailings from the Sea View parcel were submitted for assay or further testing and were reported as containing or returning 31 oz 15 dwt, indicating very substantial gold remained after ordinary crushing. The exact analytical versus recovered basis must be checked in the original report table.
- The warden described Barmundoo reefs as generally small and patchy. In the higher tableland country, gold at depth became associated with refractory ore for which ordinary battery treatment was considered almost useless.

The importance of Barmundoo is therefore not merely its grade. It documents a specific failure mode: rich but narrow shoots entering refractory sulphide ore, with substantial values left in tailings. Mabel and Sea View are high-priority mine-plan and metallurgical-recovery records.

The same annual report anticipated reopening the **Figtree reefs on Dumgree Station** once the Mount Rainbow battery began operating. Those reefs had reportedly been abandoned because ore had to be carted about 20 miles to a mill. They remained uneconomic under those costs when the yield declined to 30 dwt/t, approximately **46.7 g/t**. The upper figure in the OCR phrase describing the earlier grade is unreadable and is not adopted. Even its stated declining grade was high in geological terms, making Figtree a strong transport-stranded lead.

### Golden Cave identity resolved at Crocodile

The previously unnamed 1897 Crocodile parcel of 40 tons for 120 oz can now be identified as the **Golden Cave**.

- The March 1897 field article explicitly calls Golden Cave the adjoining mine and states that its 40-ton crushing yielded **120 oz**, exactly **3 oz/t or 93.3 g/t Au**.
- An earlier July 1889 return records 30 tons from Golden Cave producing 35 oz 10 dwt of smelted gold, approximately **1.183 oz/t or 36.8 g/t**.

Two high-grade parcels separated by roughly eight years materially strengthen the record. Golden Cave is promoted from an unnamed class C lead to **class B**. The next task is to determine which adjoining mine supplied the spatial reference in the 1897 article and match both to the Crocodile workings map.

### Queen of the Valley near Mount Morgan

Queen of the Valley provides another high-grade satellite lead but contains a reporting discrepancy that must be preserved.

- A contemporary 1895 report places the mine about two miles south-east of **Struck Oil**.
- The mine manager reported 22 tons for 50 oz, approximately **2.27 oz/t or 70.7 g/t**.
- The mill return recorded only 12 tons for 34 oz 3 dwt, approximately **2.85 oz/t or 88.5 g/t**.

The figures may represent different accounting boundaries, selected consignments or a reporting error. The mill return is the stronger grade evidence, but neither should replace the other until the battery book or warden table is recovered. The relative description to Struck Oil is suitable for mine-plan reconstruction but not for a modern coordinate.

### Norton, Bompa and Mount Larcom annual-report leads

The 1895 annual report clarifies why some Gladstone fields have weak production records despite numerous workings.

- At **Norton**, reefing was in abeyance while a small chlorination plant was being erected. This supports the interpretation that refractory ore treatment, rather than absence of gold, constrained at least part of the field.
- At **Bompa or Mount Jacob**, the Standby, Dream, Garden and Whetters claims were held by two parties. Each party was erecting a small battery because the Norton mill was about 22 miles away over a poor road. Only a picked parcel had previously been sent to Norton.
- At **Sneaker's Gully, Mount Larcom**, Golden Fleece was under exemption after a disappointing crushing and water failure. Golden Rose Block reportedly gave a satisfactory return, but no tonnage or recovered gold appears in the retrieved text.
- A **23 oz nugget** was shown from near the head of Fire Creek at Raglan. The Raglan storekeeper bought 130 oz of alluvial gold during 1895 and believed another 100 oz was taken away by Chinese miners.

These are not yet high-grade reef records, but they explain the historical bias in the surviving data: distant mills, water shortages and refractory treatment caused miners to select only the richest parcels and abandon lower-grade or mineralised stone.

1. Queensland unquestionably contained reef shoots returning several ounces per tonne.

## Pass 7: workings, aliases and spatial control

### A new district-scale control map

The 1910 Geological Survey map *East Central Queensland: gold, mineral and coal fields* has now been added as the principal historical framework for the current spatial reconstruction. It was compiled by W. H. Greenfield at 1:250,000 and combines field boundaries, parish and selection boundaries, roads, railways, creeks and an index of then-current leases.

This map materially improves the investigation, but it does not locate individual old shafts with survey-grade precision. Its correct use is as a bridge between narrative descriptions and larger-scale mine plans:

| Lead | What the 1910 sheet contributes | Remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Rosewood Champion | confines the search to the Rosewood Goldfield and preserves Five Mile Creek, neighbouring creeks, roads and cadastral selections | the Champion working itself is not labelled at this scale |

### Rosewood Champion: stronger reconstruction, but an important company-name trap

The June 1898 warden report now provides a compact working model rather than merely a spectacular crushing:

- prospectors: Kele and Read;
- legal description: prospecting protection area No. 72, subsequently described as Rosewood Champion R.C.;
- ground: 400 ft by 400 ft, comprising a 300 ft by 400 ft reward claim and two 100 ft by 400 ft ordinary claims;
- locality: head of Five Mile Creek, about five miles south of Rosewood township, in dense scrub and rugged country;
- lode: approximately east-west, very steep to near-vertical, soft ironstone with rich veins;
- working: a diagonal easterly sink to about 53 ft, with walls becoming more defined around 45 ft;
- formation: about 2 ft 8 in to 3 ft thick at the principal exposure;
- verified mill parcel: 4 tons for 68 oz 16 dwt, or 17 oz 4 dwt per ton;
- access constraint: stone had to be packed to Rosewood, then carted to Blackfellow's Gully, about 20 miles to the nearest battery.

The warden also recorded a north-west-side mineral lode at the bottom of the sink, interpreted at the time as decomposing sulphide, possibly copper and iron, with an assay pending. That is geologically important because it suggests the visible free-gold ironstone may have been part of a mineralised structure rather than an isolated surface pocket. The reported 15-20 oz/t and 30 oz/t figures were dish prospects, however, not bulk mill returns.

A later corporate trail needs to be treated cautiously. By 1900 the Rosewood Champion company was purchasing the old Annie mine at Cawarral and moving plant there. Therefore later references to the company, plant or directors do not automatically describe work at the original Five Mile Creek discovery. This appears to be a corporate migration or acquisition, not evidence that Rosewood Champion was geographically at Cawarral.

Spatial confidence is now **field and creek-head scale, medium-high**. Exact shaft location remains unconfirmed pending a reward-claim or cadastral plan that shows P.P.A. 72/R.C. boundaries.

### Golden Cave: an alias family and a numbered claim

The Crocodile-field records distinguish at least three related names:

- Golden Cave P.C.;
- Golden Cave Extended;
- Golden Cave West United.

An August 1892 notice identifies **Golden Cave West United as claim 934, Crocodile G.F.**, held or worked by Thomas Davidson and party. An April 1892 labour return records six men, 14 ft driven, 24 ft sunk and 12 tons raised at West United, while separately mentioning Golden Cave P.C. This proves that the names were not merely interchangeable newspaper spellings.

The existing rich returns must therefore remain attached to the wording used by each source until the mine plan shows which parcel came from which claim. In particular, the 40 tons for 120 oz and the later 30-ton parcel should not yet be silently pooled with West United's workings.

The most promising spatial source is the Queensland State Archives item *Map of environs of Mount Morgan to accompany report on Mount Morgan and other gold mines on Crocodile Goldfield*, by Government Geologist Robert L. Jack. If its claim numbers are legible, claim 934 may provide the first defensible mine-scale anchor for the Golden Cave group.

### Mount Usher: legal geography and a historical-production discrepancy

The 1897 report places the Mount Usher mines on the Mount Morgan Goldfield in the **parish of Bouldercombe, county of Livingstone, district of Port Curtis**. This is a substantially better cadastral search key than the broad modern label "Mount Morgan" and points directly to the GSQ 1973 Bouldercombe mine map, record mr010758.

Modern exploration reporting describes a 3.8 km shear-hosted quartz-lode corridor incorporating Mount Usher, Caledonia-Anglo Saxon and Victor. It also reports 125 historical rock-chip re-assays, 19 above 1 g/t Au, with individual results of 24.1, 16.7, 15.2 and 14.5 g/t Au. Those data corroborate a mineralised corridor, not the continuity or width of an orebody.

There is also a production-accounting issue to resolve. One modern announcement gives historical production of 30,250 oz from 29,762 tons, while broader company summaries refer to roughly 100,000 oz from combined alluvial and hard-rock sources. These figures may describe different geographic and mining scopes. They should not be treated as competing estimates of the same mine until their source tables are recovered.

Present-tenure screening remains decisive: the Mount Usher corridor is reported within MDL 2020. Historical evidence therefore does not imply public detecting, sampling or access rights.

The leading cases divide into three different investigation types:

| Type | Best examples | Next decisive evidence |
|---|---|---|
| spectacular narrow shoot | Rosewood Champion, Mabel | claim plan plus later development and crushing history |
| repeated production from a cluster of adjoining claims | Golden Cave group, Queen of the Valley | mine-scale Crocodile map and claim-number reconciliation |

Company names moved, claims were extended or amalgamated, and the same reef could be reported under a prospecting claim, an extended claim and a later company. Mine-name matching without claim numbers is now the principal risk of false attribution.

## Pass 8: legal-access screening and Clermont reconstruction

### Mount Morgan No. 7 Dam GPA

Queensland's 2026 official map confirms a legally recognised general permission area around Mount Morgan No. 7 Dam. The mapped GPA is not the entire reserve: the reservoir, dam infrastructure, perimeter buffer, roads, cycleways and other hatched exclusions remove a substantial part of the ground. The remaining permissible land occurs mainly as upland blocks north, east, south-east and west of the waterbody.

The practical target model must therefore be residual and eluvial ground on permitted slopes, benches and weathered-rock surfaces. Gullies and creeks are explicitly excluded, even though they would ordinarily be attractive gold traps. Within the legal footprint, priority should be given to safe low-gradient surfaces containing quartz or ironstone float and subtle breaks in slope, while respecting the mapped buffers and current signs.

This is now an **A-grade access prospectivity area** in the limited sense that both mineral potential and general fossicking permission are officially documented. It is not evidence that gold will be recovered at any particular point.

### Four Mile East and West: the strongest newly resolved Clermont lead

An October 1865 report describes **Hurley's Four-mile Rush** near Clermont. A prospector sank a 5 ft by 3 ft shaft to 22 ft and recovered a reported parcel of nuggety gold, including one piece of about one ounce. The payable flat was said to extend for roughly two miles.

The current Four Mile East GPA map contains **Hurley Creek**, while the connected Four Mile West map preserves the neighbouring Wait-A-While, Sandy, Rolf and Scurvy Creek drainage network. The concurrence of the Hurley name, Four Mile locality and payable-flat description makes Four Mile East the strongest presently legal-access historical reconstruction in this pass.

This match remains an inference until the 1902 Clermont contour and mine-workings map independently places Hurley's rush. The original gold occurred at about 22 ft depth, far beyond recreational fossicking limits. The legal modern target is therefore not the deep lead itself but:

- shallow reworked material where erosion intersects the lead margin;
- eluvial gold on slopes immediately above the old flat;
- benches and interfluves retaining Tertiary or younger wash;
- permitted ground around, but not within restricted stream-bank zones;
- surface nugget dispersion detectable without deep excavation.

Four Mile East and Four Mile West currently appear substantially less obstructed by mapped mining leases than McDonald Flat, but the official maps warn that applications and approvals can change. GeoResGlobe must still be checked immediately before field use.

### McDonald Flat: downgraded on access, not geology

The 2026 official McDonald Flat GPA map shows large parts of the nominal permission area overlapped by **ML 100492** and **ML 100521**. Fossicking within those lease areas requires written authority-holder permission. This makes McDonald Flat a poor default public recommendation despite its historical name and favourable gully setting.

Any CQ Diggings presentation should mask the mining-lease overlaps rather than showing the complete GPA as uniformly accessible. Remaining unencumbered fragments require current-tenure confirmation because the map itself warns that it may not reflect later applications or approvals.

### Flat Diggings

Flat Diggings is a comparatively compact GPA south-east of Four Mile East. Its official map shows internal tracks and proximity to Bath, Hurley and Slaty creeks but does not itself show a mining-lease overlap. Its strongest role may be as a continuation or reworking area downstream or across the structural/drainage divide from the Four Mile system. That interpretation requires terrain and historical-map testing and is not yet a verified gold trend.

## Pass 10: geological target model and revised field priority

### The Clermont gold is not one simple placer system

The strongest academic evidence divides Clermont gold into two broad types:

1. structurally controlled lode gold, mostly in the Bathampton Metamorphics; and
2. gold at the base of Permian conglomerate basins.

David Wood's ANU thesis concludes that the conglomerate-hosted occurrences include both transported palaeoplacer nuggets and later hydrothermal gold. Gold is concentrated in and beside fractures cutting the unconformity between Permian sediment and the underlying Anakie Metamorphic Group. This means that a modern search based only on creek bends or old alluvial names is incomplete. The more diagnostic target is the conjunction of basal conglomerate, basement contact and cross-cutting structure.

This interpretation is consistent with the older government description of Cement Hill, Hurley's and the Four-mile. The historical section at Cement Hill records 15 to 30 ft of tough conglomerate, with the bottom 4 or 5 ft forming the auriferous wash. The reported average for that auriferous part was about 5 to 6 dwt per ton, approximately 7.8 to 9.3 g/t. This is a historical reported average, not a modern compliant resource estimate. The same source says the drift at Hurley's and the Four-mile was of the same character.

### Why Black Ridge moves to first place

Black Ridge now has four independent lines of support:

- a separate rush occurred there in 1896;
- a cultural heritage study, drawing on district histories, says gold was fairly easily obtained from shallow diggings in the late 1890s;
- contemporary and later reports document auriferous dirt and continuing gold output; and
- modern academic work identifies the Black Ridge unconformity and fractures through it as a specific mineralisation setting.

The official July 2026 GPA map shows ML 100159 across the north-west, but leaves a substantial southern and eastern area outside that mapped lease. That open part should not be treated as uniformly prospective. Within it, the highest-priority field indicators are:

- exposed or shallow basal Permian conglomerate near its contact with metamorphic basement;
- quartz-rich residual gravel derived from weathered conglomerate;
- fractures, lineaments or abrupt steps crossing the basement-conglomerate contact;
- intersections between differently oriented fracture sets;
- shallow benches and slope wash immediately below eroding conglomerate remnants; and
- disturbed shallow-diggings ground outside ML 100159, provided it is inside the current GPA and not otherwise excluded.

Manganese-coated quartz or quartzite clasts, historically called "pilot stones", are reported in specialist descriptions of Black Ridge. They are a useful geological clue, but not proof that gold is present. No rock should be excavated from a face or working, and heritage features and shafts must not be disturbed.

### Four Mile East remains the best deep-lead reconstruction

The 1865 Hurley's Four-mile report and the modern Hurley Creek name remain a strong spatial match. The older geological account adds that Hurley's and the Four-mile contained the same style of ancient conglomeratic drift as Cement Hill. This strengthens the geological correlation even though the missing 1902 map still prevents exact alignment.

The original reported shaft depth of about 22 ft makes the central deep lead a poor recreational target and places it outside normal shallow fossicking. McDonald Flat has stronger historical support than the earlier access ranking implied. The evidence includes:

## Pass 19: drainage and source-to-trap validation

The final Clermont screen added the Queensland Government watercourse-line dataset and tested named drainage within one kilometre of every target polygon. Drainage was used as supporting context, not as an excuse to colour every creek gold.

The principal named relationships are:

- Brigalow field site 13803 with Brigalow Creek;
- Four Mile East with Hurley Creek;
- Birimgan West with Leahy Creek;
- Birimgan East fault-contact ground with Scurvy Creek;
- Four Mile West high-level wash with Rolf Creek and Wait-A-While;
- Bathampton young alluvium with Bath Creek and Sandy Creek;
- Flat Diggings with Bath Creek and Hurley Creek;
- McDonald Flat with McDonald Gully; and
- Museum Reserve with Two Mile Creek and Wolfang Creek.

The absence of a named stream beside the Birimgan East recorded alluvial-mine sector, Black Ridge pocket, McMasters target or Town Reserve target does not reject them. Those targets are controlled by mapped workings, old wash, conglomerate, pits or structural geometry rather than a named modern channel.

No target was upgraded solely because a creek crosses or approaches it. Modern drainage can rework and disperse gold from older deposits, but it can also cut across the real palaeodrainage direction. The correct field use is to inspect permitted shallow benches, residual wash, interfluve edges and erosional windows. Active channels, gullies and stream-bank toes remain poor legal recommendations under the applicable conditions.

The published drainage layer contains only watercourses within one kilometre of the target polygons, retaining names, perenniality, hierarchy and stream order where supplied. It is a context layer rather than a prospectivity layer.

## Pass 20: desktop field-validation points

The target polygons answer a regional question. They do not tell a field user how to test the reasoning without wandering about hopefully until lunch. Pass 20 therefore created one conservative desktop validation point inside each of the fifteen ranked sectors.

Each point was selected with a coarse 45 by 45 internal grid. The retained grid cell maximised approximate distance from the target boundary. This produced approximate edge clearances from 22 metres to 250 metres. None of the fifteen points intersects a mapped current-tenure exclusion.

The points are deliberately labelled as validation locations rather than gold coordinates. Their job is to test whether the mapped geology, wash, quartz gravel, contact or historical disturbance can actually be recognised on permitted ground. Every point carries:

- its parent target and confidence;
- approximate clearance from the target boundary;
- named drainage context where present;
- a target-specific observation or small-test task;
- a same-day legal gate; and
- an instruction to move or abandon the point when signage, tenure, hazards or exact-ground conditions disagree with the desktop screen.

Active channels, shafts, pits, banks, infrastructure and tenure exclusions remain outside the recommended procedure. A point that survives a desktop calculation can still lose an argument with a fence.

## Status

Pass 20 converts the fifteen ranked Clermont sectors into a repeatable field-validation programme. The research layer contains twenty-four validated prospectivity and exclusion features, a separate official-watercourse context layer and fifteen conservative desktop validation points. All twelve listed Clermont GPAs have now been screened at detailed scale. The remaining uncertainty is empirical: whether controlled tests on currently lawful ground support, weaken or reject the desktop ranking.
