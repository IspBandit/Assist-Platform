Short answer

Yes, there is prospectable ground. No, the whole GPA is not necessarily it.

The strongest currently mapped shallow lead is the Birimgan East sector around a government-recorded alluvial gold mine and recent workings. Brigalow is next because field site 13803 explicitly records alluvial gold in a creek exposure. Black Ridge follows because a small open pocket sits nearest the proven Permian conglomerate system.

McDonald Flat remains useful, but only in fragments. Three current gold lease applications occupy most of its GPA. The two old-mine observations are inside that excluded ground, so the public layer shows only alluvial remnants outside the applications, with an additional 50 metre research setback.

Investigation process

How a broad gold story became a field map.

Each stage could reject, downgrade or narrow a target. A promising name was not enough.

1

Start with evidence

Historical production, Queensland field sites, old workings, geological reports and official GPA maps.

2

Rebuild the legal footprint

Intersect the official GPA with current ML and MC applications and grants. Excluded means excluded.

3

Test the gold model

Young alluvium, high-level wash, basal Permian conglomerate, metamorphic contacts, faults and recorded quartz gravel.

4

Check drainage

Use named creeks and gullies to understand transport and reworking. A creek is not a conveyor belt with a delivery docket.

5

Publish only the surviving polygon

Targets, exclusions and drainage remain separate. Rectangular search boxes never become access boundaries.

Using it in the field

The six checks before the detector leaves the vehicle.

  1. Licence: carry a current Queensland fossicking licence.
  2. Tenure: check the exact point in GeoResGlobe on the day.
  3. Boundary: confirm you are inside the official GPA and inside the target polygon.
  4. Ground: stay out of claims, leases, applications, signed exclusions and closed tracks.
  5. Infrastructure: keep clear of buildings, roads, utilities, water facilities, gates and stock infrastructure.
  6. Method: use permitted hand tools and detectors, refill holes, and do not excavate gullies or stream-bank toes.
The coloured polygon is a research conclusion, not a survey peg. GPS error, data currency and on-ground signs can all matter. When the map and the sign disagree, the sign wins and the detector gets a short rest.
